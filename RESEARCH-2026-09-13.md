# What the outside world says — research, 2026-09-13

Open web only (X is not indexable from here; the X pass is the Grok prompt in
`design/GROK-PROMPT.md`). Every number below has a source at the bottom.
Written before the social accounts exist, on purpose: this is the foundation
check the owner asked for, and it changes three things.

## 1. The venue is bigger and hotter than our record shows

- pons has produced **~646,000 tokens from 167,000+ creator addresses** since
  July; **~25,000 launches a day** on 2 September; **$544M trading volume**
  and **$5.95M in fees in one day** — more than pump.fun ($4.64M) that day.
  Robinhood Chain opened to the public on **1 July 2026**.
- PONS ran ~18,000% from its July low; **29% of supply burned**.
- Top native tokens: Cash Cat $254M, Goose $78M, Chump $30M.

**What this means for us.** Our canonical record starts 5 September and holds
~168k launches. That is a week of a venue that has done 646k. Every
"all-time" figure on the site is honest about its window, but the phrase
"all-time" will read as a claim about the venue's life to a visitor who
knows the venue. **Change: say "since 5 September" wherever we say
"all-time", and backfill the record to pons v2's first launch (14 August)
now that the box exists** — Bitquery's archive confirms that is where v2
begins.

## 2. The mechanics we describe have a piece we did not know about

pons v2 has a **snipe tax: 99% on buys at launch, decaying to zero over 5
seconds** (≈25% at 1 s, ≈3% at 2 s), collected back into the launch, not
burned. The docs say why: *"A bot watching for new curves can buy the
opening of one before anybody else has seen it exists, then sell into the
people who arrive a minute later."* There are at least two open-source pons
sniper bots on GitHub.

**What this means for us.** It sharpens, not weakens, the headline:
- A graduation **inside 5 seconds** happened *through* a 99%→3% tax — whoever
  did it was willing to lose most of the buy to the launch itself. That is
  not a trader; that is someone whose goal was the graduation, not the
  tokens.
- A graduation at **6–10 seconds** is the race the instant the tax hits
  zero — bots, competing.
- Our "under 10 s" bucket therefore contains two populations the mechanics
  separate at 5 s. **Change: add the 5-second edge to the histogram and the
  ladder, and say what the snipe tax means for each side of it.** That is a
  definition change, so it is a dated `METHOD.md` entry and a new bucket,
  not a relabel.
- The pre-registration's commitment 1 (the creator wallet never buys) now
  also reads as: we will not be on the wrong side of the venue's own
  anti-snipe rule.

**What this means for us.**
- **Our "moat" data is purchasable.** Bitquery sells the post-graduation
  series we are collecting, back to 14 August, for $49/month. `PLAN.md` risk
  4 said the head start is not a moat; this is that risk realised in a
  week. What Bitquery does not sell is the *published, defined, tested
  statistic* — nobody has to compute a median or state an n to read our
  page. That is the product; the collection is the input.
- **Everyone else scores.** ponsscan's 0–100 safety score is the exact
  thing `CONSTRAINTS` 1 forbids us. That is now a visible difference, not a
  private principle: three sites tell you which token is safe, one tells
  you what the population did. **Change: say this on the front page, in one
  line** — "no scores; the population, with n" — because a visitor arriving
  from a scorer needs to know why we look different.
- **ponsscan.com's wallet-claiming** (link an X handle to a wallet) is a
  feature we cannot copy under `CONSTRAINTS` 2 and should not want to.
- **Their n=12 safety cohort** is the kind of thing our method page exists
  to be compared against. Do not attack it; publish ours beside it.

## 4. Tool tokens elsewhere: what held and what did not

- **Bubblemaps (BMT)** — the best-known analytics token. Launched with a
  real product and a stated utility (vote on investigations); trades at
  $0.014 on ~$1.2M daily volume in March 2026. A working product did not
  stop the token from being a small, quiet asset.
- **GMGN, Photon, Axiom, BullX, Padre** — the tools traders actually use
  are **terminals that execute trades**, and they monetise on fees, not on
  a token. The 2026 framing from launchpad coverage: the contest has moved
  from "who launches fastest" to "who can tell organic demand from bots."
  That is our sentence, said by the market.

**What this means for us.** The honest read is unchanged from `PLAN.md`: a
data token is a lottery ticket on distribution, and the room is most of the
odds. The market evidence adds one thing: **the tools with durable users
are the ones a trader acts *through*, not just reads** — which is exactly
what Phase 3 (the agent) is, and why the order site → token → agent was
right. The token's job is to fund the thing traders would actually use.

## 5. Gaps in our foundation this research exposes

1. **No V2-era backfill.** The record should start 14 August, not 5
   September. Now feasible on the box.
2. **The 5-second snipe-tax edge** is absent from our buckets. Add it.
3. **"All-time" copy** overstates the window to anyone who knows the venue.
4. **No statement of difference on the front page.** Four sites now; ours
   is the only one that does not score. Say so.
5. **The X pass is not done.** The Grok prompt covers it: complaints,
   wishes, the loud accounts, tool-token outcomes on pons itself.
6. **Bitquery is a cheaper path to our own inputs.** Not for the site — a
   $49 dependency on a third party is the opposite of "reads the chain
   itself" — but worth knowing as the audit path: anyone can re-derive our
   numbers from it, which is a strength if we say so on `/method`.
